Do creatine gummies break a fast?

Plain creatine monohydrate has essentially zero calories and doesn't meaningfully spike insulin, so it doesn't break a fast in most protocols. Creatine gummies are a different question — they're a candy delivery format, and the added sugar in a full serving (roughly 2 to 18 g depending on brand) is a real caloric and insulin trigger that plain powder doesn't carry.

Why plain creatine doesn't break a fast

Creatine monohydrate contains no calories on its own and does not trigger a meaningful insulin response when taken alone — it is not a carbohydrate, protein, or fat, and your body does not process it the way it processes food. For most intermittent fasting protocols (16:8, OMAD, and similar), plain creatine dissolved in water, black coffee, or unsweetened tea is broadly considered compatible with the fasting window, since it does not interrupt the metabolic state fasting is meant to create.

Why gummies are a different case

A creatine gummy is a candy carrying a supplement, and the sugar is the ingredient that matters for a fasting question — not the creatine. Across the category, a full serving can carry several grams of sugar depending on the brand and piece count. That sugar carries calories and can trigger an insulin response, which is precisely what most fasting protocols try to avoid during the fasting window.

If your fasting goal is strict metabolic fasting (autophagy-focused, or a medically supervised fast), a sugared gummy is not equivalent to plain creatine powder in water — treat it as you would any small snack, and take it inside your eating window instead.

Do sugar-free gummies solve this?

Partially, and it depends on your fasting definition. Sugar-free gummies typically substitute a sugar alcohol (maltitol, sorbitol, or erythritol) for sugar. These generally carry fewer usable calories than real sugar and have a smaller insulin effect for most people, which brings them closer to fasting-compatible — but they are still not chemically identical to plain, additive-free creatine powder, and some sugar alcohols can cause GI discomfort, which is its own reason to be cautious about taking several grams of one on an empty stomach.

If strict fasting compatibility matters to you specifically, plain creatine monohydrate powder dissolved in water remains the more conservative choice during a fasting window, regardless of which gummy brand you use the rest of the day.

What to actually do if you fast and use gummies

  • If your fasting protocol is flexible (most people doing 16:8 for general health, not autophagy-focused extended fasting), taking a gummy serving is unlikely to meaningfully undermine your goals — the sugar amount is small relative to a full meal.
  • If your fasting protocol is strict (autophagy-focused, medically supervised, or you track insulin response closely), take your creatine gummies inside your eating window instead, or switch to plain powder in water during the fast.
  • Either way, consistency matters more than timing. Creatine saturation builds over weeks from average daily intake, not from precisely when in the day you take it — so shifting your gummies to your eating window costs you nothing in terms of creatine's actual effectiveness.

Questions

Does creatine break a fast?

Plain creatine monohydrate, taken alone in water, does not meaningfully break a fast for most intermittent fasting protocols — it has no calories and doesn't significantly spike insulin on its own.

Do creatine gummies break a fast even though creatine doesn't?

The sugar in a creatine gummy can. A full serving carries roughly 2.5 to 4+ grams of added sugar depending on the brand, which does carry calories and can trigger an insulin response — unlike plain creatine powder in water.

Are sugar-free creatine gummies fasting-safe?

Closer, but not identical to plain powder. Sugar-free versions use sugar alcohols, which generally carry fewer calories and a smaller insulin effect than real sugar, but they aren't chemically the same as additive-free creatine.

What's the safest way to take creatine gummies while intermittent fasting?

Take them inside your eating window if your fasting protocol is strict. Creatine saturation depends on consistent daily intake over weeks, not exact timing, so shifting gummies to your eating window doesn't reduce their effectiveness.
