The dose math: how Create compares to powder

The single biggest issue in the creatine gummy market is under-dosing. Many brands market "5,000mg per serving" on the front label, only for buyers to realize a serving requires eating 6 to 8 sugar-loaded gummies to get there.

Create takes a more transparent approach: 1.5 g of creatine per gummy, so a standard daily serving of 3 gummies delivers 4.5 g of pure creatine monohydrate. Four gummies (a "performance serving") gets you to 6.0 g.

Clinical research generally supports a daily intake of 3 to 5 g of creatine monohydrate to fully saturate intramuscular phosphocreatine stores over time. At 3 gummies, Create sits at the top of that clinical range in seconds, with no scoop or shaker involved.

Lab testing & NSF Certified for Sport: why it matters

Independent testing across the gummy category has repeatedly turned up quality-control failures — multiple rounds of HPLC testing have found that a large share of commercial creatine gummies do not meet their label claim, with some delivering little to no active creatine after heat exposure during production.

  • Creapure® raw material. Sourced from Alzchem Trostberg GmbH in Germany, widely cited for high purity and low levels of creatinine (the degradation marker for creatine that has broken down on the shelf).
  • NSF Certified for Sport. An ongoing third-party programme — not a one-off test — that screens production batches for label-claim accuracy, heavy metals, and over 280 substances banned in sport.

Taste, texture & flavor rankings

Create uses a pectin-based matrix rather than animal gelatin. That gives the gummies a firmer, less-sticky chew that holds up better in a hot gym bag, car console, or carry-on than a softer gelatin gummy.

Cost-per-dose breakdown: are Create gummies overpriced?

Let's address the price directly. A 45-gummy pouch (15 servings) runs roughly $24.99–$37 on a one-time purchase, which works out to about $1.67–2.47 per serving. Bulk unflavored creatine monohydrate powder, by comparison, costs closer to $0.20–0.30 per 5 g serving.

Why the premium can still make sense: the adherence math

Creatine only produces strength, lean-mass, and cognitive benefits when it's taken consistently. If a $20 tub of powder gets skipped three or four days a week because of shaker cleanup, chalky texture, or travel logistics, muscle saturation drops back toward baseline. The premium on Create is, in effect, buying convenience that supports daily adherence — worth it only if inconsistency is genuinely what's been holding you back.
