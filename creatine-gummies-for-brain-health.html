Creatine gummies for brain health and memory

Creatine is now sold for focus as much as for muscle. Here is what randomized trials measured for memory, attention and processing speed, and what that means for a daily gummy.

The short version. A 2024 meta-analysis of 16 randomized trials (492 adults) found creatine monohydrate gave a small improvement in memory (moderate-certainty evidence) and possibly faster attention (low to very low certainty) and processing times (low certainty). It did not improve overall cognition or executive function. The trials used 3–20 g a day, so a gummy only makes sense if it delivers a real, verified dose every day.

Why creatine could affect the brain at all

Your brain uses about a fifth of the body’s energy. Creatine and phosphocreatine act as a fast energy buffer in brain cells just as they do in muscle. Supplementing raises brain creatine much less than muscle creatine, which is one reason brain results are smaller and less consistent than strength results. Researchers think the effect shows up most when the brain is under stress: sleep deprivation, older age, low dietary creatine (vegetarians and vegans) or illness.

A corrigendum was published in 2025. Participants were aged about 21–76; doses ranged from 3 g/day to 20 g/day; durations from a single dose (~7.5 hours) to 18 months.

An SMD around 0.3 is a small effect. In everyday terms: some people may notice nothing, and nobody should expect creatine to work like caffeine. It is a slow, daily-use supplement, not a same-day focus aid.

Who seems to benefit most

  • Women: in the meta-analysis subgroup, processing-speed gains were significant in women and not in men. Separately, an 8-week trial in perimenopausal and menopausal women found faster reaction time with creatine HCl. See creatine for menopause.
  • Older adults: several reviews report memory effects concentrated in older participants. See creatine for older adults.
  • Vegetarians and vegans, who get little creatine from food. See vegan creatine gummies.
  • People who are short on sleep: small lab studies show creatine can blunt some sleep-deprivation effects. This is early work.

Dose, and why the gummy part matters

The brain trials mostly used creatine monohydrate at 3–5 g a day for weeks, and some used much higher amounts. Nobody knows the best dose for cognition yet. What is clear is that an underdosed product can’t reproduce even the small effects measured in trials, and gummies are the format most likely to be underdosed: in three public testing rounds, 16 of 28 gummy tests missed their label claim, some by more than 99%.

So the checklist for a “brain” gummy is the same as for a strength gummy:

  1. At least 3–5 g creatine monohydrate per labeled serving (check with our calculator).
  2. Third-party certification on the exact flavor, or a recent public potency test.
  3. A price you can sustain for months, because the trials ran for weeks to months.

How to read “boosts focus” claims on gummy packs

Many creatine gummy brands, Create included, mention cognition or memory in their marketing. Those claims draw on the general creatine literature, not on trials of the specific gummy. That is normal for supplements, and legal, but it means the product label proves the dose, not the outcome. Treat any percentage “memory improvement” figure as a summary of other people’s studies.

Questions

Do creatine gummies help with brain fog?

Creatine monohydrate has small, measurable effects on memory in pooled trials, and possible effects on attention and processing speed. No trial has tested a creatine gummy for brain fog specifically, and gummies fail potency tests more often than powder.

How much creatine do you need for brain benefits?

Trials used 3 g to 20 g a day. The best dose for cognition is not established. Most people take 3–5 g of creatine monohydrate daily, which is also the dose used for muscle.

How long does creatine take to affect memory?

Trials ranged from a single dose (~7.5 hours) to 18 months, and the meta-analysis found no clear difference between shorter and longer durations. Brain creatine rises slowly, so plan on several weeks of consistent use.

Is creatine good for focus during work or study?

It is not a stimulant and will not feel like caffeine. Any effect builds with daily use and is small on average. It may matter more when you are short on sleep.

Do women get more brain benefit from creatine?

One meta-analysis subgroup found processing-speed gains in women but not men, and small trials in menopausal women are promising. The evidence is early and based on few participants.
