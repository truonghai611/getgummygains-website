How the maths works

There are only two operations, and doing them in your head at the shelf is a genuinely useful skill:

  1. Total creatine in the jar = gummies per jar × grams per gummy. This is the only quantity you are actually buying.
  2. Real doses in the jar = total creatine ÷ 5. Divide the price by that and you have a number you can compare against anything, including powder.

Worked example. A jar at $34.99 with 60 gummies at 1 g each contains 60 g of creatine — twelve real 5 g doses. That is $2.92 per dose, and the jar runs out in twelve days, not the "30 servings" on the front. Roughly ten times the cost of powder.

The second output matters as much as the first. If a product needs six or more gummies for a full dose, look honestly at whether you will chew six every morning in week eight. Most people do not, which converts a fairly-priced product into an expensive placebo.

Questions

How do I calculate the real cost of creatine gummies?

Multiply gummies per jar by grams of creatine per gummy to get total creatine in the jar. Divide that by 5 to get the number of real 5 g doses. Then divide the price by that number. The result is directly comparable to powder and across brands.

What is a reasonable cost per 5 g dose for a gummy?

Roughly $1.10–1.70 is normal for an honestly dosed gummy. Above $2 you are paying 6–10x powder. Below $1 is unusual and worth double-checking against the supplement facts panel, because it often means the label is quoting creatine per gummy rather than per serving.

Why does the number of gummies per dose matter so much?

Because adherence collapses as piece count rises. A 3-gummy dose is a habit; an 8-gummy dose is a chore, and people quietly cut it to four. At that point the product is not underdosed — your intake is.
