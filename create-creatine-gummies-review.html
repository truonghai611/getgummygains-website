The conflicting lab results, in full

We think this is the single most important thing to understand about this product, and almost no review mentions it.

NOW Foods (earlier round, 13 brands, HPLC). Create was listed among the brands that did not meet label claim. NOW specifically noted that Beast Bites, Create and Con-Cret showed large amounts of creatinine — the marker of creatine that has degraded on the shelf.

SuppCo (June 2025, ISO 17025-accredited lab). Create's watermelon gummies were one of only two gummies out of six to pass, measuring 4.59 g per serving against a 4.5 g label — above claim. SuppCo did note that both passing gummies still showed significant creatinine, which it attributed to the format rather than the brand.

How we read it. The rounds are separated in time, use different lots, and Create has since taken NSF Certified for Sport — an ongoing programme rather than a one-off test. That is a meaningful improvement in verification. It is still a category where degradation is real, so buy recent stock and do not stockpile.

The dose math

1.5 g per gummy is the highest per-piece dose we have found in a certified product, and it is why the serving is only three gummies. Most competitors need five to eight.

4.5 g is marginally under the 5 g most commonly used in studies. In practice this is a non-issue — the ISSN range is 3–5 g and saturation is a function of daily intake over weeks. If you want to sit at exactly 5 g, four gummies gives you 6 g and burns through the bottle faster.

What it really costs

A 45-gummy bottle is 15 servings, not 45 days. At $24.99 that is about $1.67 per serving; at $37 it is closer to $2.47. Against bulk powder at roughly $0.20 per 5 g, you are paying an 8x to 12x premium for convenience and certification.

Who should buy it

Buy it if the thing keeping you off gummies is not knowing whether the creatine is actually in there. NSF Certified for Sport is the strongest answer available in this category, and three gummies a day is genuinely easy to sustain.

Skip it if cost per gram is your main criterion, or you are happy taking powder. You are paying a large multiple for verification and convenience, and that is only worth it if you value those things specifically.

Questions

Are Create creatine gummies legit?

The verification behind Create is the strongest in the gummy category: NSF Certified for Sport, Creapure sourced creatine monohydrate, and an independent ISO 17025 lab measured 4.59 g against a 4.5 g label claim in 2025. It is worth knowing that an earlier NOW Foods round listed Create among brands not meeting claim, with elevated creatinine — so you are looking at two contradictory results from different dates.

How many Create gummies should I take?

Three gummies, which is 4.5 g of creatine monohydrate at 1.5 g per gummy. A 45-gummy bottle is therefore 15 days, not 45.

Is Create worth the price?

At roughly $25–37 for 15 servings, you are paying about $1.67–2.47 per 4.5 g dose — around five to eight times bulk powder. The case for it is the certification and the low three-gummy piece count, not the economics.

What does NSF Certified for Sport actually mean?

It is an ongoing third-party programme that tests batches for label accuracy and for over 280 substances banned in sport, and maintains a public registry you can search. It is materially stronger evidence than a brand saying it is 'lab tested'.
