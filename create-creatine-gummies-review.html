The conflicting lab results, in full

We think this is the single most important thing to understand about this product, and almost no review mentions it.

NOW Foods (earlier round, 13 brands, HPLC). Create was listed among the brands that did not meet label claim. NOW specifically noted that Beast Bites, Create and Con-Cret showed large amounts of creatinine — the marker of creatine that has degraded on the shelf.

SuppCo (June 2025, ISO 17025-accredited lab). Create's watermelon gummies were one of only two gummies out of six to pass, measuring 4.59 g per serving against a 4.5 g label — above claim. SuppCo did note that both passing gummies still showed significant creatinine, which it attributed to the format rather than the brand.

How we read it. The rounds are separated in time, use different lots, and Create has since taken NSF Certified for Sport — an ongoing programme rather than a one-off test. That is a meaningful improvement in verification. It is still a category where degradation is real, so buy recent stock and do not stockpile.

Other Ingredients, verbatim: Tapioca Syrup, Sugar, Allulose, Reverse Osmosis Water, Pectin, Natural Watermelon Flavor, Natural Color, Citric Acid.

Two things that list settles. The gel is pectin, not gelatin, so the product is consistent with a vegan diet. And there is no sugar alcohol in it — no maltitol, sorbitol or erythritol — which is the ingredient class most associated with gas and bloating in gummy supplements.

The sugar figure nobody had published

Create is not marketed as sugar-free, but until now no independent page carried its actual numbers. They are 5 g of total sugars per serving, of which 2 g are declared as added sugars, inside 8 g of total carbohydrate and 60 calories. Over a 30-day tub that is 1,800 calories and 150 g of sugar you would not get from powder or capsules.

One detail we will flag without speculating about: the panel’s own two sugar figures leave a 3 g gap it does not explain. Allulose is not the explanation — under FDA final guidance allulose is excluded from both Total Sugars and Added Sugars, so it should appear in neither number. We are reporting what the label says rather than guessing at the formulation behind it.

Those are the numbers to compare against other brands, not the per-serving price, because the servings are not the same size.

The dose math

Three gummies is the whole serving — one of the lowest piece counts we have found in a certified product, where most competitors need five to eight. What those three gummies contain is no longer in question: the printed Supplement Facts panel on the tub we bought reads 4.5 g of creatine monohydrate (1.5 g per gummy) — see the photograph above.

Either figure works. 4.5 g is marginally under the 5 g most commonly used in studies, and in practice that is a non-issue — the ISSN range is 3–5 g and saturation is a function of daily intake over weeks, not of any single dose. If you want to sit at exactly 5 g, a fourth gummy takes you to 6 g and burns through the tub faster.

The rest of the Create line, and how the gummies fit in it

Create sells the same creatine in three formats, and the gummies are the most expensive gram-for-gram of the three. Worth knowing before you default to them:

  • Gummies — 1.5 g per gummy, 3 gummies per serving, 90-count tub = 30 servings.
  • Unflavored stick packs — a full 5 g of creatine monohydrate per packet at a stated 99.9% purity, no sugar, nothing to chew. Listed in the NSF registry as “Create Wellness Unflavored”.
  • Creatine + Electrolytes stick packs — 5 g creatine, roughly 800 mg electrolytes and 1,000 mg taurine per stick, no sugar and no caffeine. A 30-stick box lists at $60, or $48 on subscription. The flavour range now includes a Lemonade Collection (Classic, Peach, Raspberry and Strawberry Lemon, plus variety packs) on that same unchanged formula — each flavour separately listed in the NSF Certified for Sport registry.

If the reason you want gummies is that you will not mix a powder, the gummies are the right call and the premium is what you are paying for. If you would mix something, the stick packs give you the full 5 g research dose instead of 4.5 g, for less.

What women ask most often before starting

Three questions come up repeatedly, and all three have straightforward answers:

  • “Will it make me look bulky?” Creatine does not raise androgen levels. It increases the water held inside muscle cells, which is a different thing from subcutaneous puffiness.
  • “Will I bloat?” The retention is intracellular. A scale increase of roughly 1–2 lb in the first two weeks is normal and is water in muscle, not fat gain.
  • “Is the dose different for women?” No. The 3–5 g daily target in the ISSN position stand applies to healthy adults regardless of sex.

What does NSF Certified for Sport actually mean?
