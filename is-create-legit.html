Is Create legit?

Create is a real U.S. company selling creatine gummies, and some flavors are NSF Certified for Sport. Public lab results are mixed: one 2025 test measured 4.59 g against a 4.5 g label, and an earlier round did not meet the claim. Two lawsuits allege underdosing. Neither docket we checked shows a judgment on the merits.

What “legit” can and cannot mean

Create passes the basic tests of a real brand. Create Wellness sells creatine gummies, takes payment on its own checkout, and has specific gummy flavors in the public NSF Certified for Sport directory. That is enough to say the company and the product line are real.

It is not enough to say every serving matches the label. Gummy creatine has failed label checks often enough, across several brands, that one certification and one passing sample cannot close the question. The rest of this page is the evidence we can actually point to.

The label does not say one number

Source check — October 14, 2026: Create’s Blue Raspberry product page described 1.5 g per gummy and 4.5 g per three-gummy serving, while the Create homepage advertised 5 g in three gummies. An archived Watermelon tub photo from September 15, 2026 shows 4.5 g of creatine monohydrate per three-gummy serving, 5 g of total sugars, and the Creapure and NSF marks on the front. That photo is a label reference, not a new lab test and not proof we authenticated the purchase. The full transcription is on the Create review.

What the public tests show

Three published rounds are the ones this site tracks. Create appears in more than one of them, with different results. The product-by-product table is on the lab test tracker.

  • NOW Foods, earlier HPLC round. Create was among the gummies that did not meet the label claim. NOW also noted creatinine, a breakdown product, in Create, Beast Bites, and Con-Cret. That round is older than the NSF listing and the 2025 test below.
  • SuppCo, June 2025, ISO 17025 lab. Create’s watermelon gummies were one of two gummies in that round reported as a pass: 4.59 g measured against a 4.5 g label. SuppCo still reported significant creatinine and treated that as a gummy-format issue, not a Create-only finding.
  • NSF Certified for Sport. This is an ongoing product certification, not a one-time potency test of every batch you might buy. Confirm the flavor in the NSF gummy records we checked, and in the live directory, before you treat “NSF” on a homepage as applying to the SKU in the cart.

A passing sample and a failing sample can both be real. They were different lots, different dates, and different labs. Buy recent stock, and do not treat a 2025 pass as a test of the bag that arrives next month.

The underdosing lawsuits, as filed

Last updated: October 14, 2026. Two complaints allege that Create creatine gummies contained less creatine than a labeled 4.5 g serving. The paragraphs below report what those filings and one published company response say. They are not a finding about the pack you would buy.

On April 22, 2026, John Connolly filed Connolly v. Create Wellness, Inc., No. 7:26-cv-03324, in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York. The complaint alleges that an independent ISO/IEC 17025 laboratory measured 4.01 g of creatine per serving in a sample of orange gummies. The public docket, checked October 14, 2026, lists a first amended complaint on June 11, 2026. The entries posted through June 18, 2026 do not include a judgment.

A separate complaint, Macartney v. Create Wellness, Inc., No. 5:25-cv-10713, was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California on December 16, 2025. It alleges that July 2025 testing measured 3.62 g of creatine monohydrate per serving. The same docket records a notice of voluntary dismissal on May 20, 2026, and lists the case as terminated on May 27, 2026. A voluntary dismissal is not a court finding about the test result. The complaint PDF is posted by Truth in Advertising.

Asked about the New York complaint, Create co-founder and CEO Dan McCormick told NutraIngredients on April 30, 2026 that the company disputes the allegations. He said Core Create gummies undergo creatine potency testing on every production order through Eurofins, are NSF Certified for Sport, and that Create’s review of tests for the gummies the plaintiff bought showed creatine at or above the label claim. That is the company’s account of its own testing.

The same lawsuit summary is on the Create review and the best creatine gummies page.

A practical answer

Create is legit in the narrow sense shoppers usually mean: it is a real U.S. brand, the gummies exist, and specific flavors carry NSF Certified for Sport. It is not “cleared.” An earlier public test missed the label, a later public test passed a 4.5 g claim, storefront copy has disagreed with itself about 4.5 g versus 5 g, and two complaints allege underdosing without a merits judgment in the records above.

If you buy it, match the Supplement Facts, the NSF record, and the checkout total for the exact flavor and pack. The Create review holds the label photo and the longer evidence table. The price check is a dated comparison, not a live quote.

Questions

Is Create a real company?

Yes. Create Wellness sells creatine gummies in the United States, and specific gummy flavors are listed in the NSF Certified for Sport directory. A real company and a certified product are not the same thing as proof that every tub matches its label.

Did independent tests find Create gummies underdosed?

The record is mixed. NOW Foods listed Create among gummies that missed the label claim in an earlier HPLC round. A 2025 ISO 17025 test reported by SuppCo measured 4.59 g against a 4.5 g label. The lawsuits allege lower results from other samples. One test does not describe every batch.

Have the Create lawsuits been decided?

Not in the records checked on October 14, 2026. The New York case, Connolly v. Create Wellness, Inc., had no judgment in the docket entries through June 18, 2026. The California case, Macartney v. Create Wellness, Inc., was voluntarily dismissed and terminated on May 27, 2026. A dismissal is not a ruling that the test was right or wrong.
