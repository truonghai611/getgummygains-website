The 3 biggest creatine myths for women, busted by science

Despite being one of the most researched ergogenic aids in sports nutrition, these myths still keep women away from creatine.

Myth 1: "Creatine will make me look bulky or masculine."

Female physiology produces significantly less testosterone than male physiology, and creatine does not raise androgen levels. What it does is fuel the ATP energy system, letting you complete an extra rep or two during strength training, pilates, or HIIT — which over time supports firm, lean muscle definition rather than bulk.

Myth 2: "Creatine causes severe bloating and stomach cramps."

The water retention from creatine happens inside muscle cells (intracellular), which is what makes muscles look hydrated and toned rather than puffy. Bloating or stomach discomfort tends to show up with cheap, poorly purified powders, or when someone jumps straight into a large 20g "loading dose."

Myth 3: "It's only for hardcore bodybuilders."

Research has pointed to benefits for women around bone density, joint resilience, and hormonal health across different life stages, well beyond bodybuilding contexts.

Cognitive energy: banishing brain fog & afternoon crashes

One area of growing interest in nutritional research is creatine's role in brain energy metabolism. The brain uses a large share of the body's total metabolic energy, and when you're sleep-deprived, stressed, or multitasking, cerebral phosphocreatine levels can deplete quickly.

  • Daily supplementation with around 4.5 g of Creapure® supports faster ATP recycling in neural tissue.
  • Many women report sharper morning focus and steadier afternoon energy without relying on extra caffeine — though individual response varies.
