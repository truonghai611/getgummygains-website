Beast Bites creatine gummies review

A full 5 g dose, eight flavours in the NSF Certified for Sport registry, and the cheapest per-serving price of any certified gummy we have priced. It also appeared on the wrong side of an independent lab round. Both things are true and the order you read them in matters.

Ingredients as published: creatine monohydrate, tapioca syrup, cane sugar, water, pectin, citric acid, natural flavours, natural colours, sunflower oil, carnauba wax. It contains cane sugar — this is not a sugar-free product, and the brand does not publish a sugar figure per serving where a shopper can see it. Free shipping applies on subscriptions and orders over $70.

One inconsistency worth noting before you buy: the brand states 30 servings per pack, but sells both a 75-count and a 150-count pack at different prices. Those cannot both be 30 servings at 5 g. The 150-count works out cleanly at five gummies per serving; treat the 75-count as fifteen servings and price it accordingly, at roughly $1.67 each.

The certification is real, and unusually broad

We searched the NSF Certified for Sport registry directly on 13 September 2026. Beast Bites returns eight creatine gummy listings: Hawaiian Fruit Punch, Pink Lemonade Squeeze, Simply Sweet Strawberry, Sour Peach Rings, Sour Watermelon Splash, Sweet Blue Raspberry, Sweet Strawberry and Tropical Mango Citrus. For comparison, Force Factor returns six and Lemme returns three. Only Create, at 25 listings across three formats, is broader.

That matters because certification attaches to a specific product and flavour. A brand with one certified flavour out of eight can still print the badge on its homepage. Beast Bites is not doing that — the range really is covered.

The part that complicates it

An earlier independent HPLC round run by NOW Foods on thirteen creatine gummy brands listed Beast Bites among the products not meeting label claim, with elevated creatinine — the breakdown product that forms when creatine sits in water and heat. Create and Con-Cret were flagged in the same round. We report this because we report it for every brand, including the one we recommend.

Two honest caveats in Beast Bites’ favour. That round is the oldest of the three we track, and a result on a batch from one point in time is not a permanent verdict on a product — formulations, suppliers and manufacturing controls change. And unlike some brands in that round, Beast Bites has since entered an ongoing certification programme, which is a materially different posture from doing nothing.

What we cannot do is tell you it has been re-tested and passed. It did not appear in the later SuppCo or James Smith/Eurofins rounds, so there is no published independent measurement of a current batch. The full testing record is here.

Our honest read

On paper this is a strong product: the full 5 g dose that Create does not quite reach, the broadest flavour-level certification after Create, and the lowest certified price per serving in the category. If price is your deciding factor and you want a certified product, Beast Bites is a rational pick and we will not pretend otherwise.

What keeps it off our recommendation list is the evidence gap rather than the old flag itself. Create has two published independent lab results on recent batches, one of which measured over claim. Beast Bites has a certification and an unresolved older result. For a category where the central failure mode is exactly the thing that round found, that difference is the whole argument.

Questions

Is Beast Bites NSF Certified for Sport?

Yes. The NSF Certified for Sport registry returns eight Beast Bites creatine gummy listings when searched directly — Hawaiian Fruit Punch, Pink Lemonade Squeeze, Simply Sweet Strawberry, Sour Peach Rings, Sour Watermelon Splash, Sweet Blue Raspberry, Sweet Strawberry and Tropical Mango Citrus (checked 13 September 2026). That is broader flavour coverage than Force Factor or Lemme.

How much creatine is in Beast Bites gummies?

Five grams of creatine monohydrate per serving, brand-stated, which meets the standard dose used in most research. The brand states 30 servings per pack; on the 150-count pouch that works out to five gummies per serving.

How much do Beast Bites cost per serving?

The 150-count pouch is $39.99, or $35.99 on subscription — about $1.33 a serving, or $1.20 subscribed. That is the cheapest per-serving price of any NSF-certified creatine gummy we have priced. The 75-count tub at $24.99 works out closer to $1.67 a serving.

Did Beast Bites fail a lab test?

An earlier independent HPLC round run by NOW Foods listed Beast Bites among creatine gummy products not meeting label claim, with elevated creatinine. Create and Con-Cret were flagged in the same round. Beast Bites did not appear in the two later published rounds, so there is no independent measurement of a current batch either confirming or clearing that result.

Are Beast Bites gummies sugar-free?

No. The published ingredient list includes cane sugar and tapioca syrup. The brand does not publish a sugar figure per serving. They are pectin-based rather than gelatin-based, so they are vegan, and are marketed as non-GMO and made in the USA.
