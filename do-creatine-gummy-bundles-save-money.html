Do Create Creatine Gummy Bundles Save Money?

Affiliate disclosure: We may earn a commission if you buy through our Create links, at no extra cost to you. Our disclosure.

Yes. Every multi-month tub bundle costs less per serving than buying a one-month tub each month — on subscription and one-time alike. The saving is real but modest: about 4% on the two-month bundle and about 17% on the five-month one. Two earlier conclusions on this page were wrong and have been removed: bundles are still sold, and subscription does not beat every bundle.

The comparison that changes the answer

Five one-month subscriptions at $48 would cost $240 for 150 servings if that price held. The High Five subscription supplies the same 150 servings for $199 — $41 less, about 17% — but asks for the whole $199 up front instead of $48 a month. One-time against one-time, the same bundle is $249 for 150 servings ($1.66 a serving) against $300 for five separate $60 tubs ($2.00 a serving).

The curve flattens at the top. The five-month bundle is the cheapest per serving on subscription, and the six-month one is fractionally more expensive per serving, not less — paying $41 more up front buys no further discount.

What this table does not cover. Flavor line-ups differ between bundles of the same length, and every pouch bundle costs more than the tub bundle with the same number of servings. We have not tested a checkout, so renewal pricing after the first delivery, shipping and tax are not included here.

Choose based on how you actually buy

  • Trying a flavor for the first time: a smaller order reduces the amount committed before you know you like it.
  • Already know you will use the product: compare the multi-month subscription with repeated one-month purchases, including renewal prices and delivery frequency.
  • Do not want recurring orders: compare one-time prices with one-time prices; a subscription saving creates an ongoing order arrangement.
  • Lowest creatine cost matters most: include plain powder in your comparison. Gummies charge for convenience and taste.

Check the total before paying

  1. Confirm tub versus pouch, flavor and serving count. A tub's count is gummies; pouch counts may refer to sachets.
  2. Confirm one-time versus subscription, delivery frequency and the next renewal price.
  3. Read the actual discount line, shipping and tax. The roughly 20% already shown in the table is the product page’s own subscription plan discount, available to anyone who subscribes — do not deduct a further 20% unless the cart shows it on your selection.
  4. Divide the final total by labeled servings. For products with different serving amounts, also compare cost per gram.

Questions

Do Create bundles save money compared with a monthly subscription?

Yes, every tub bundle does, by between about 4% and 17% per serving. On 18 September 2026 the five-month High Five tub bundle was $199 on subscription for 150 servings, against $240 for five $48 monthly tubs — about 17% less, before shipping and tax.

Is the 20% an extra affiliate discount?

Not as far as we can verify. Create’s product pages attach a 20% discount to the subscription plans themselves (15.7% to 20.1% on the longer bundles), and that is the discount our table already shows. We have not confirmed any further affiliate-only reduction at checkout, so do not plan on one.

Is a 450-count tub bundle 450 servings?

No. At three gummies per labeled serving, 450 gummies provide 150 servings. Check whether a count refers to individual gummies or pre-portioned sachets.

Should I buy the largest bundle?

Not automatically. The six-month bundle costs more per serving than the five-month one, so the largest order is not the cheapest. A lower cost per serving also means a larger payment up front and more product to get through before the flavor choice is settled.
