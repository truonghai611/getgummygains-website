Are creatine gummies FSA/HSA eligible?

Usually not, and the reason is worth understanding rather than just accepting. Here's what IRS rules actually say about general wellness supplements, and the specific paperwork that can change the answer.

The general rule

Under IRS Section 213(d), FSA and HSA funds can be used for expenses primarily intended to treat or prevent a specific medical condition. General health, wellness, and fitness products — which is how creatine, a performance and general-health supplement, is normally classified — typically fall outside that definition on their own. This is the same reason a gym membership or a multivitamin usually isn't FSA/HSA eligible by default, even though both have genuine health value.

What can actually change the answer

A Letter of Medical Necessity (LMN) from a doctor, tying the supplement to treatment of a specific diagnosed condition, can sometimes make an otherwise-ineligible product reimbursable. This isn't a workaround anyone can request casually — it requires an actual clinical rationale from a provider, tied to your specific medical situation, and plan administrators can still decline it. If you have a documented medical reason a doctor is willing to put in writing, ask your FSA/HSA administrator about their specific LMN process before purchasing.

Some FSA/HSA administrators maintain their own eligible-expense lists that are more specific than the general IRS guidance, and these can vary by employer and plan provider. If you're unsure, your plan's online portal or customer service line is the authoritative source — not a product page's marketing claim.

Watch for marketing claims on product pages that say a product is "FSA/HSA eligible" without qualification. Some retailers integrate with third-party eligibility-checking tools at checkout, which can flag a product as eligible under specific conditions that may not apply to your situation. When in doubt, verify with your own plan directly rather than trusting a checkout widget.

The bottom line

For most people, creatine gummies purchased for general fitness or performance purposes are not FSA/HSA eligible without a Letter of Medical Necessity from a doctor tying the purchase to a specific diagnosed condition. That's not a creatine-specific limitation — it's the same rule that applies to most general wellness and performance supplements.
