Methodology of this report

Two independent testing programmes are the basis for every number on this page:

  • NOW Foods (HPLC testing): 13 creatine gummy brands purchased and tested by high-performance liquid chromatography. 6 met their label claim; 7 did not.
  • SuppCo (ISO 17025-accredited lab): 11 products purchased off the shelf — 5 powders and 6 gummies. All 5 powders passed. 4 of the 6 gummies failed, two with no detectable creatine.

GummyGains did not commission either round. We compiled, cross-referenced, and cite both directly. Testing dates and lots differ between rounds and formulations change over time — treat every figure as a dated data point, not a permanent verdict on a brand. Three findings worth quoting

  1. Amazon star ratings did not predict a lab pass. A top-rated bestseller in the SuppCo round measured 0.005 g of creatine against a 5 g label claim — roughly 2,000 gummies for one real dose.
  2. Gummies are the only creatine format where labs found brands with functionally zero active ingredient. Every powder tested in the same SuppCo round passed.
  3. A brand's own "lab tested" claim carried no weight in these results. One brand advertised its own certified testing and then failed independent testing twice, on two different lots.

Questions people actually ask

What is the pass rate for creatine gummies in independent testing?

Across the two public testing rounds compiled here, 46% of tested creatine gummy brands met their label claim for elemental creatine per serving. NOW Foods found 6 of 13 brands passed by HPLC; SuppCo found 2 of 6 gummies passed via an ISO 17025-accredited lab, versus 5 of 5 powders in the same round.

Did GummyGains run these lab tests?

No. This report compiles and cites two independent, publicly reported testing programmes — NOW Foods and SuppCo. GummyGains did not commission either round. If we ever commission our own testing, we will disclose the method and lab used.

Why do creatine gummies fail more often than powder?

Gummies are manufactured with water and heat, and creatine slowly converts to creatinine in the presence of water over time. A gummy can be correctly dosed on the production line and still be underdosed by the time it reaches a customer — a failure mode dry powder does not share.
