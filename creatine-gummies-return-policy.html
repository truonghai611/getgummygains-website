Can you return creatine gummies if you don't like them?

A month of creatine gummies costs more than most people spend on a supplement they have never tasted. We read Create's actual return, shipping and subscription terms on 12 September 2026 rather than summarising the marketing page. The short answer changes how you should place the first order.

The headline: Create does not accept returns on its gummies — even unopened and sealed — citing the ingestible nature of the product. If an order arrives damaged, incorrect or unsatisfactory you have 30 days from delivery to contact them, and the outcome (full refund, partial refund, replacement or store credit) is at the company's discretion. Why this matters more here than in most categories

Flavour is the single most subjective thing about a creatine gummy, and it is the one variable no review can settle for you — including ours, which is why we do not claim to have tasted products we have not bought. Combine that with a no-returns policy and a one-month box that lists at $60, and the first purchase carries real risk of being $60 you cannot get back on a texture you dislike.

That is not a reason to avoid the category. It is a reason to structure the first order differently from the tenth.

The cheap way to find out whether you like them

Create sells single-serve packets for $1 — individual gummy servings in seven flavours, and individual Creatine + Electrolytes sticks in three. For the price of a coffee you can settle the flavour question before committing to a month. Almost nobody does this, because the store does not put it in the main navigation.

The next step up is the legacy 45-count tub at $27.99, which is 15 servings rather than 30. It is still on sale but no longer linked from the main menu, and stock varies by flavour. If the $1 singles are unavailable in the flavour you want, that tub is the smallest real commitment.

Shipping, and the $100 line

Shipping is free on every subscription order. One-time orders qualify for free shipping once the cart passes $100. That threshold is worth knowing before you decide between a one-time box and a subscription: at a single 90-count tub, a subscription is both cheaper per serving and free to ship, while the equivalent one-time order is neither.

What the subscription actually commits you to

Create sends a reminder email before each subscription order processes, and any change — flavour, size, date, cancellation — has to be made before that order processes. Orders are typically processed within one business day. Two consequences follow from the policy text, and both catch people out:

  • Cancelling does not cancel an order that has already been processed. If your subscription was active when the order went through, that order stands.
  • Once processed, modification is not guaranteed — so the reminder email, not the renewal date, is the real deadline.

None of this is unusual for a subscription supplement. It is just rarely written down anywhere a buyer reads it before the second charge.

How to check any brand's policy in two minutes

Before a first order with any supplement brand, open three pages: the refund or return policy, the shipping policy, and the subscription terms if you are subscribing. You are looking for four specific things — whether unopened product can be returned, the number of days you have to raise a problem, whether resolution is guaranteed or discretionary, and what cancelling does to an order already in flight. Brands that are comfortable with their answers make those pages easy to find.

Bottom line. Treat the first creatine gummy purchase as a flavour test, not a supply run: $1 singles first, or the smallest tub you can find. Once you know you will actually eat them every day, the subscription is both the cheapest per serving and the only way to get shipping thrown in.

Questions

Can you return Create creatine gummies?

No. Create's published return policy states that because the gummies are ingestible, returns are not accepted for safety and quality-control reasons, even if the product is unopened and sealed. The only route is to contact them within 30 days of delivery if the order arrived damaged, incorrect or unsatisfactory, in which case a full refund, partial refund, replacement or store credit may be offered at their discretion.

What if the order arrives damaged or wrong?

Contact Create within 30 days of delivery. The policy allows for a full refund, partial refund, replacement or store credit, chosen by the company according to the nature of the issue. Orders placed via Amazon are limited to a replacement, and orders placed through an external site or retail partner cannot be refunded by Create at all.

Is there a cheap way to try the flavours first?

Yes. Create sells single-serve packets at $1 each — seven gummy flavours and three Creatine + Electrolytes flavours. There is also a legacy 45-count tub at $27.99, half the size of the current 90-count box, though it is no longer linked from the main navigation and stock varies by flavour.

Does cancelling a subscription stop an order that is already processing?

No. Create's policy states that cancelling a subscription does not cancel an order that has already been processed, and that orders typically process within one business day. A reminder email goes out before each renewal; that email, rather than the renewal date itself, is the practical deadline for changes.

Do you get free shipping?

Free shipping applies to every subscription order. One-time orders need to reach $100 before shipping is free, which a single 90-count tub does not.

Is a no-returns policy normal for supplements?

It is common for ingestible products, and the reasoning — a sealed tub cannot be verified as untampered once it leaves the warehouse — is legitimate. What varies between brands is the window for reporting a problem, whether resolution is guaranteed or discretionary, and how easy the policy is to find. Read those three things before a first order rather than after.
