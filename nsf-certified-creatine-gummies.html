What the registry is, and why it is different from “third-party tested”

NSF Certified for Sport is an ongoing certification programme, not a one-off test. To stay listed, a product is audited at the facility level, tested batch by batch for label accuracy, and screened against more than 280 substances banned in sport. The part that matters for a shopper is the last step: every certified product appears in a public database anyone can search by brand or product name.

“Third-party tested” on a label means none of that by itself. It can mean a single paid test, run once, on one batch, with a certificate the brand is under no obligation to publish. Both phrases sound like verification. Only one of them leaves a record you can check without asking the brand's permission.

What a zero in that table does not mean

It does not mean the product is contaminated, underdosed, or unsafe. It means one specific, checkable form of evidence is absent. A brand can run excellent testing and simply not enrol in this programme — certification costs money and takes time, and for a small brand the maths may not work.

What the zero does mean is that you are being asked to take the brand's word for it. In a category where independent rounds have repeatedly found products missing their label claim, that distinction is the whole point. Our position is not “never buy an uncertified gummy.” It is: know which one you are buying, and price it accordingly.

The flavour-level detail most reviews skip

Certification attaches to a specific product, and often to a specific flavour. A brand with one certified flavour out of six can still say “NSF Certified for Sport” on its homepage without technically lying. That is why the table above lists flavours rather than brands.

If you are buying on the strength of the certification, check your exact flavour rather than the brand.

How to check this yourself in about a minute

Go to the NSF Certified for Sport product search, type the brand name, and read the results. If your exact product and flavour is not there, it is not certified under this programme, whatever the packaging says. Do it at the moment of purchase rather than trusting any review, including this one — listings are added and removed over time, and this page is a snapshot of one day.

Questions

Which creatine gummies are NSF Certified for Sport?

As of 12 September 2026, four brands on our review list have creatine products in the NSF Certified for Sport registry: Create Wellness (25 listings covering every gummy, electrolyte and unflavored stick-pack flavour it sells), Momentous (8, including Creatine Chews in Lemon Lime, Mango and Strawberry), Force Factor (6 gummy listings) and Lemme (3). Legion, Bloom Nutrition, SWOLY, Wild Gainz and Bounce Nutrition returned no creatine listings.

Is “NSF Certified for Sport” the same as “third-party tested”?

No. NSF Certified for Sport is an ongoing programme with facility audits, batch-level label-accuracy testing, screening for over 280 banned substances, and a public registry you can search. “Third-party tested” is an unregulated marketing phrase that can describe a single paid test on one batch, with no obligation to publish anything.

Does a missing NSF listing mean the product is bad?

No. It means one checkable form of evidence is absent, not that the product failed anything. Certification is voluntary and expensive, and plenty of good products skip it. The practical consequence is that you are relying on the brand's own account of its testing rather than on a record you can look up.

Do I need to check the specific flavour?

Yes. Certification is granted at product level and often flavour by flavour, so a brand can advertise the badge while only part of its range is listed. Search the registry for the exact flavour you are about to buy.

How often does the registry change?

Continuously. Products are added as they are certified and removed when certification lapses. This page records what the search returned on 12 September 2026; check the live directory before buying rather than relying on any dated snapshot.

Is NSF Certified for Sport required for drug-tested athletes?

It is not legally required, but it is the standard most testing bodies and team nutritionists point athletes toward, because the banned-substance screening is the part of the programme designed for exactly that risk. If you are subject to testing, the registry search is worth doing every time you reorder, not once.
