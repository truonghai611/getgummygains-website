How we picked

Before recommending any creatine gummy, we check four things: how much elemental creatine is actually in a full serving (not per gummy — brands love hiding behind "per gummy" numbers), whether it's creatine monohydrate (the only form with a real research base) or a novelty blend, how much added sugar you're signing up for, and whether the brand publishes third-party testing.

Before you buy any creatine gummy, check the math

The single most important number on the label isn't "creatine per gummy" — it's total elemental creatine per full serving. Most gummies pack 1–2g of creatine per piece, meaning you need 4–8 gummies to reach the standard 3–5g daily dose used in research. A bottle that looks cheap per-unit can be expensive once you count the gummies needed to hit an actual dose. See our full dosage guide for where the 3–5g target comes from and whether a loading phase is worth it.

Quick check before you buy: Divide the bottle price by (grams of creatine per gummy × gummies in the bottle) ÷ 5. That's your real cost per 5g dose — the number that's actually comparable across brands and against powder.

What we're not including — and why

We chose not to list larger legacy supplement brands' gummy lines here where the per-serving creatine dose was unclear or required an unusually high gummy count to reach 5g. If a brand doesn't make that number easy to find, we don't recommend it, regardless of how well it's marketed.
