What you're actually paying for

A large tub of loose powder is the cheapest way to buy creatine per gram — you're paying almost entirely for the raw ingredient. Stick packs cost more because someone else already did the measuring: no scoop, no scale, no shaker to rinse. That premium only makes sense if the prep step is genuinely the thing standing between you and a daily dose.

Who this is for

  • People who already drink coffee, water, or a shake daily and just need to add something to it
  • Anyone wanting the exact 5 g clinical dose without counting gummies or pieces
  • Buyers cutting added sugar who don't want a flavored gummy format
  • Frequent travelers who don't want a tub taking up bag space — see our full travel comparison

Who should look elsewhere

  • If cost per gram is your main criterion and you buy in bulk, a large loose-powder tub is meaningfully cheaper
  • If you want zero liquid involved at all, gummies remove that step entirely — see gummies vs. stick packs, side by side

How to verify before you buy

  1. Confirm the label lists creatine monohydrate at 5 g — not a blend, and not a smaller "per scoop" number disguised as a full serving.
  2. Search the NSF Certified for Sport database for the exact product name; NSF lists Create's unflavored creatine as a separate entry from its gummies and electrolyte SKU.
  3. Confirm current price per packet and whether the seller offers one-time purchase or subscription-only — these terms change, so check the product page directly rather than trusting any number in a review, including ours.
