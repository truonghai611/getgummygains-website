The cross-check nobody else runs: how many certified creatine gummies exist at all

The answer is four brands. On 15 September 2026 the NSF Certified for Sport registry held 2,501 certified products. 259 of them contain creatine. Exactly 37 of those are gummies, and they belong to four companies: Create Wellness (15 listings), Beast Bites (13), Force Factor (6) and Lemme (3). No other brand in the entire programme has a certified creatine gummy.

Most coverage of this category checks whether a particular brand is certified. That is the wrong question, because it cannot tell you how unusual certification is. Searching the registry by product form answers it: of every creatine product NSF has certified, roughly one in seven is a gummy, and those come from four companies out of the dozens selling in the category.

Two details worth carrying:

  • Certification and lab failure are not mutually exclusive. Beast Bites holds 13 certified gummy listings and was flagged for elevated creatinine in the NOW round. Certification audits a facility and a batch; it does not follow a jar through its shelf life.
  • Powder listings do not cover gummies. Several brands hold certified creatine powders or chews while none of their gummies are listed. The badge on a homepage is brand-level marketing; the registry is product-level evidence.

Method: the NSF Certified for Sport product search was queried on 15 September 2026 and every returned listing recorded, then filtered to listings carrying both the creatine ingredient tag and the gummy product form. Counts are of distinct registry listings, so a brand selling the same flavour under two registry entries counts twice. Anyone can reproduce this at the NSF Certified for Sport product search. Full brand-by-brand detail, flavour by flavour, is on our NSF registry check.

Why the gummy format fails so often

Creatine monohydrate is chemically stable as a dry powder and stays that way for years in a sealed tub. Put it in water and it slowly converts into creatinine — an inert waste product your body simply excretes. Gummy manufacturing uses water and heat by definition, and then the product sits on a shelf for months.

That is why a gummy can be honestly formulated, pass a factory test, and still arrive underdosed. It is also why the fix is not "trust bigger brands" but "look for ongoing third-party certification and recent batch data".

The number almost nobody publishes: impurity limits

Every test round above asks the same question — is the creatine in there at all? There is a second question that almost no consumer brand answers in public: what else came along with it.

Creatine monohydrate is made industrially, and the two byproducts the trade actually worries about are dicyandiamide (DCD) and dihydrotriazine (DHT). Both are manufacturing residues rather than deliberate ingredients, and both are controlled by specification at the raw-material stage — which means the ceiling is set long before a gummy is moulded, by whoever the brand buys its powder from.

On its quality page, Create publishes numeric ceilings: DCD at or below 50 ppm and DHT at or below 3 ppm, alongside a claim of ≥99.9% purity and a statement that batches are tested for purity, potency and label accuracy at launch and again over shelf life to check stability.

What we could and could not verify. Those figures are Create’s own published specification, not an independent measurement, and we have not seen a certificate of analysis for a specific batch. What we can say is narrower and still useful: of the brands on our review list, Create is the only one we found publishing numeric impurity ceilings on its own consumer site at all. We checked Legion and Force Factor and found no equivalent figures. A published limit is not proof; it is a commitment a brand can be held to, and the rest of the category is not making one.

The practical use of this is in comparison, not in isolation. If you are weighing two certified products at similar prices, the one that publishes a number has told you something the other has not. If a brand publishes neither a lab result nor a specification, “pharmaceutical grade” on the tub is a phrase, not a measurement.

How to read any of this without getting burned

  • Check the date of the test. The NOW round is the oldest of the three; SuppCo and the James Smith / Eurofins round both landed in mid-2025. Create failed the NOW round and then passed both later rounds. Formulations change. An old failure is a reason to look for a newer result, not an automatic verdict.
  • Prefer ongoing certification over one-off tests. NSF Certified for Sport and Informed Sport re-test batches continuously and publish searchable registries.
  • Ignore star ratings entirely. All four products that failed the SuppCo round had strong Amazon ratings, and together sold roughly 50,000 units in a single month.
  • Ask for a batch COA. Any brand confident in its product will send one. The response time tells you a lot on its own.

Where that leaves the buying decision. Applying our own rules above: Create is the brand with two consecutive newer passes (4.59 g against a 4.5 g claim in the SuppCo/ISO 17025 round, and again in the July 2025 Eurofins round) plus ongoing certification rather than a one-off test. It is also the brand that appears on the failing side of the older NOW round — all of that is on this page on purpose. Buy recent stock, and do not stockpile.

Questions about the testing

How many creatine gummies are NSF Certified for Sport?

As of 15 September 2026 the NSF Certified for Sport registry held 2,501 certified products, of which 259 contain creatine and exactly 37 are gummies. Those 37 listings belong to four brands: Create Wellness (15), Beast Bites (13), Force Factor (6) and Lemme (3). No other brand in the programme has a certified creatine gummy. Certification is granted per product, so a brand with certified creatine powder may have no certified gummy.

Which creatine gummy brands failed lab testing?

In the NOW Foods HPLC round of 13 brands, the ones reported as not meeting label claim were Astro Labs, Beast Bites, Create, Con-Cret, Greabby and Njord. In the SuppCo round, Happyummmm and DivinusLabs contained only trace creatine, and EcoWise and Vidabotan had none detectable. In the James Smith / Eurofins round (July 2025), Ovrload, Gains Nutrition, Unique Physique, Supplmnt and Push all showed borderline-undetectable creatine. Testing dates differ and formulations change — always check for a newer result before treating any of this as current.

Which creatine gummy brands passed?

NOW Foods reported Bear Balance, Bod, Effective Nutra, Iron Labs Nutrition, Peach Perfect and Zhou as meeting label claim. SuppCo's round found Force Factor at 6.3 g against a 5 g label and Create at 4.59 g against a 4.5 g label. The James Smith / Eurofins round found Wellboost, Myvitamins, Known Nutrition and Create within range of claim, with the closest overage at 5.55 g against a 5 g label. Note that Create appears on the passing side of both the SuppCo and Eurofins rounds, after failing the earlier NOW round.

Why do so many creatine gummies fail?

Gummies are manufactured with water and heat, and creatine converts to creatinine in the presence of water over time. A gummy can therefore be correctly dosed on the production line and meaningfully underdosed by the time it reaches a customer. In the NOW round, three of the six products reported as missing label claim — Beast Bites, Con-Cret and Create — were also reported with elevated creatinine. For the other three the round published no creatinine figure, so we record those as not reported rather than clean.

Does a brand's own 'lab tested' claim mean anything?

Not much on its own. EcoWise promoted its own certified testing results and then failed independent testing twice, across two different lots. Self-reported testing has no external auditor. NSF Certified for Sport and Informed Sport are the two programs that carry real weight, because both maintain public registries you can search before you buy.

What did the James Smith / Eurofins creatine gummy test find?

In July 2025, UK fitness influencer James Smith sent 9 creatine gummy products to Eurofins for independent testing. Four passed within range of their label claim (Wellboost, Myvitamins, Known Nutrition and Create), while five showed borderline-undetectable creatine (Ovrload, Gains Nutrition, Unique Physique, Supplmnt and Push). It is the third public test round to find roughly the same failure pattern in the gummy format.
