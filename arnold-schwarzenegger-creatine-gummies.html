What Arnold Schwarzenegger said about creatine gummies

The seven-time Mr. Olympia told his newsletter readers that most creatine gummies contain almost no creatine. Here is the lab round behind that warning, product by product.

By Henry Nguyen · Updated October 19, 2026 · How we evaluate

The short version. In his Arnold’s Pump Club newsletter on October 28, 2025, Arnold Schwarzenegger warned that lab tests found most creatine gummies contained almost no creatine, and recommended tested creatine monohydrate powder. The test he drew on was SuppCo’s 2025 round: 4 of 6 gummies failed, and all 5 powders passed. The two gummies that passed were Create (4.59 g measured vs 4.5 g label) and Force Factor (6.3 g vs 5 g). So his warning is right about the category, and incomplete about individual brands.

What Arnold said

The newsletter’s own heading was “The Bitter Truth About Creatine Gummies.” Fitness Volt reported the piece. As reported, he said independent tests found that most contained almost no creatine, and some none. He said a good gummy is possible to make but that the format isn’t reliable yet, and he pointed readers to creatine monohydrate powder with third-party verification such as NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport. The newsletter passages quoted by Fitness Volt do not name brands.

Source: Fitness Volt, October 28, 2025, summarizing Arnold’s Pump Club.

Is Arnold right?

About the category, yes. SuppCo’s round was not a one-off. Across the three public testing rounds we track (NOW Foods, SuppCo and a July 2025 Eurofins round publicized by James Smith), 16 of 28 gummy tests missed their label claim, while powders passed. Heat and moisture during gummy manufacturing can break creatine down into creatinine.

About every gummy, no. Two products in the very round he relied on delivered their labeled dose. Several brands also carry NSF Certified for Sport on specific flavors. A fairer summary is: most cheap, uncertified gummies are a gamble; a certified gummy with a published passing test is a reasonable choice if you will take a gummy and not a powder.

Caveats on the passes. One sample is one lot. Create also passed the July 2025 Eurofins round, but appears in an earlier NOW Foods round where it did not meet its claim, and two lawsuits have alleged underdosing (one was voluntarily dismissed; neither had a merits judgment in the dockets we checked). What to do with this

  • Cheapest and most reliable: plain creatine monohydrate powder, as Arnold suggests. See gummies vs powder.

  • Avoid unknown marketplace brands with no certification and very low prices.

Questions

What did Arnold Schwarzenegger say about creatine gummies?

In his Pump Club newsletter on October 28, 2025, he said independent lab tests found most creatine gummies contained almost no creatine, and he recommended third-party tested creatine monohydrate powder.

Which creatine gummies passed the test Arnold cited?

In SuppCo’s 2025 round, Create (4.59 g measured vs 4.5 g label) and Force Factor (6.3 g vs 5 g) passed. Four other gummies failed, two with no detectable creatine. All five powders passed.

Does Arnold recommend creatine gummies?

No. He recommended tested creatine monohydrate powder, while saying a good gummy is possible to make.

Are creatine gummies a scam?

Many failed public tests, but not all. Look for NSF Certified for Sport on the exact flavor and a published passing potency test, and expect to pay more per gram than powder.
