How to check if your creatine gummy brand has been flagged

As of this writing, no creatine gummy brand has issued a formal FDA recall. What has happened is arguably more relevant day-to-day: independent lab testing rounds (NOW Foods, SuppCo) have flagged multiple brands for not meeting label claims, and at least two brands face active class-action investigation over testing discrepancies. Here’s how to actually check a specific brand yourself.

"Recalled" and "flagged by independent testing" are two different things, and the gummy category has much more of the second than the first. A recall means a regulatory action; a testing flag means a private lab found the product didn’t match its label. Both matter, but they require checking different sources — this page walks through both.

FDA recalls: the actual status

The FDA maintains a public database of food and dietary supplement recalls. As of this writing, we found no formal FDA recall specifically for creatine gummy products. Recalls are relatively rare for supplements generally and are typically triggered by contamination, mislabeling that poses a health risk, or undeclared allergens — not simply underdosing, which is a labeling-accuracy issue handled differently (and more often through lawsuits or FTC action than an FDA recall).

Independent testing flags: the more common issue

This is where the real signal is in this category. In March 2024, NOW Foods — itself a supplement manufacturer, using its own HPLC lab — ran independent testing across 13 gummy brands and found several did not meet their label's creatine claim, with some showing elevated creatinine indicating degraded product. A separate ISO 17025-accredited round from SuppCo in June 2025 tested six gummy brands and found only two passed label-claim accuracy.

Neither of these is a recall — they are independent lab findings, published outside any regulatory process. But they are exactly the kind of evidence worth checking before buying, since they are often more current and more specific than anything a formal recall process would surface.

Active legal scrutiny

Beyond lab testing, there is active class-action investigation activity in this category. Reporting has identified potential class actions related to creatine gummy testing discrepancies, with brands including Astro Labs and Njord Creatine Monohydrate Gummies named in investigations for consumers in California, Illinois, and New York. This is separate from and in addition to the NOW Foods and SuppCo testing rounds — it reflects that the underdosing issue in this category has drawn legal, not just editorial, scrutiny.

None of the brands we currently recommend on this site (Create, SWOLY, Force Factor) have been named in the lawsuit reporting we found at time of writing — but this is an actively evolving situation, and the safest approach is to check current news for any specific brand before purchasing, not rely on a static list.

How to check your specific brand

  1. Search the NSF Certified for Sport public registry for the exact product and flavor. A confirmed listing is the strongest verification available in this category.
  2. Check the FDA recall database directly for the brand name.
  3. Search "[brand name] lawsuit" or "[brand name] lab test" for recent news — this surfaces class-action reporting and independent testing coverage that a recall search alone will miss.
  4. Look for a published, dated, third-party lab result on the brand's own site or a reputable review source — not just a "third-party tested" claim with no data attached.

Questions

Has any creatine gummy brand been officially recalled?

As of this writing, we found no formal FDA recall specifically targeting a creatine gummy product. Quality issues in this category have primarily surfaced through independent lab testing and class-action lawsuit investigations rather than regulatory recalls.

What’s the difference between a recall and a testing flag?

A recall is a regulatory action, typically for contamination or a health-risk-level labeling issue. A testing flag is an independent lab (not a regulator) finding that a product doesn’t match its label claim — more common in this category, and worth checking even when no recall exists.

Which creatine gummy brands have faced lawsuit investigations?

Reporting has identified investigations involving brands including Astro Labs and Njord Creatine Monohydrate Gummies, related to testing discrepancies. This is an evolving situation, so search current news for any specific brand before purchasing.

How can I verify a creatine gummy brand is safe before buying?

Search the NSF Certified for Sport public registry for the exact product, check the FDA recall database, and search for recent news on lab testing or lawsuits involving that brand name specifically.
