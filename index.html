Common questions

Do creatine gummies actually contain creatine?

In the selected samples we track, 16 of 28 gummy product tests missed their label claim across three public rounds. That is not an estimate of the failure rate across all gummies on sale. NOW Foods reported 7 failures among 13 products, SuppCo found 4 among 6, and the James Smith/Eurofins round found 5 among 9. The format can work, but the product and batch need credible verification.

How should I compare a gummy serving with powder?

Compare grams per labeled serving, not gummy count with scoop count. Our archived Watermelon tub lists 1.5 g per gummy, or 4.5 g in three gummies. Current Create marketing and product copy are not consistent, so check your exact pack. Powder scoop sizes and other gummy serving sizes vary. Follow the directions on the exact product; a price comparison per 5 g is not an instruction to take extra gummies.

Are creatine gummies worth the money?

Gummies may be worth a higher price if the format helps you maintain your routine. Compare the current cost per labeled serving, sugar and product-specific testing with plain powder. We have not measured whether gummies improve adherence or produce better results than powder.

What should I check on the label before buying?

Check grams of creatine monohydrate per full serving, the number of gummies in that serving, and added sugar. Then match the exact product and flavor to any certification or dated lab report. A passing sample does not guarantee every batch. Read the ingredient panel and current purchase terms before choosing.
