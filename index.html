Common questions

Do creatine gummies actually contain creatine?

Often less than the label claims. In independent HPLC testing of 13 gummy brands, only 6 met their label claim. A separate lab program found 4 of 6 gummies had effectively no creatine at all — one delivered 0.005 g against a 5 g claim. Two gummies in that same round did pass. The format can work; the average product on Amazon does not.

How many creatine gummies equal one scoop of powder?

Usually 3 to 8, depending on the brand. Most gummies carry 0.8–1.5 g of creatine each, and the research-backed daily dose is 3–5 g. Always read the serving size, not the number printed on the front of the jar.

Are creatine gummies worth the money?

They cost roughly 3–8x more per gram than powder. That premium buys one thing: adherence. Creatine only works if you take it daily for weeks, so if the gummy is the reason you stop skipping days, it pays for itself. If you already take powder without complaint, gummies are a downgrade on price and dose accuracy.

What should I check on the label before buying?

Three numbers: total elemental creatine per full serving (target 3–5 g), the number of gummies that serving requires, and added sugar across that full serving. Then look for a third-party certification such as NSF Certified for Sport or Informed Sport — not a brand's own 'lab tested' badge.
