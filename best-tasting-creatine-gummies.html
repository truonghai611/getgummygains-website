Why we're doing this differently

Our stated methodology is explicit that we don't claim to have tested products we haven't actually bought and used. Taste is the one category where nearly every competing review site quietly breaks that rule — implying personal testing through confident, specific flavor language ("bright citrus notes," "not chalky at all") without disclosing whether that's a real sensory impression or repackaged marketing copy. We'd rather tell you what the data actually is: flavor names as brands describe them, and patterns in public customer review language, where available.

What flavor variety actually looks like across the category

Berry and citrus-adjacent flavors dominate this category by a wide margin — "Berry Boost," "Orange Squeeze," "Lemon Lime," "Mango," and similar names appear repeatedly across brands, which suggests the category has converged on a fairly narrow, safe flavor range rather than differentiating much on taste. Products marketed as sugar-free sometimes carry documented customer feedback about a noticeable aftertaste from sugar-alcohol sweeteners — worth checking recent reviews for the specific product and current formulation, since brands do reformulate.

How to actually evaluate taste before committing to a full jar

  • Check for sample sizes or small counts before buying a full 30-serving jar of an unfamiliar brand.
  • Read recent verified-purchase reviews specifically, not just the star rating — taste complaints often show up in review text even on products with decent average ratings.
  • Note the sweetener type on the label. Sugar-free formulations more often rely on sugar alcohols, which some people find have a distinct aftertaste that added sugar doesn't.
  • Remember that dose and verification matter more than taste for whether the product actually does its job — see our lab-tested breakdown before choosing based on flavor alone.
