OMNI creatine gummies review

OMNI markets its creatine gummies to busy women and first-time lifters. We checked the label, the testing claims, the price per gram and the storefronts selling it.

By Henry Nguyen · Updated October 19, 2026 · How we evaluate

Affiliate disclosure: GummyGains is reader-supported. If you buy through a link on this page we may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. It does not change what we say about a product. Full policy.

The short version. OMNI creatine gummies list 3 g of creatine monohydrate per 3-gummy serving (about 1 g per gummy), below the 5 g most studies use. On October 19, 2026 we could not find a full ingredient list or sugar figure on the store pages, a linked certificate of analysis, an NSF Certified for Sport claim, or OMNI in any of the three public gummy testing rounds we track. Two storefronts use the OMNI name with different flavors, prices and review counts. Nothing here proves the gummies are underdosed, but very little proves they are not.

Two storefronts, one name. We found omnicreatine.com (support email on that domain) and a second store at omniicreatine.com (double “i”). They show different flavors, prices and review counts (from “1,838” to “3,255 verified reviews” on the second store alone), and neither lists a company address. We cannot tell whether both are run by the same company. If you buy, check the domain in your browser bar and keep your order confirmation.

Third-party “review” pages about OMNI also disagree with the brand: one lists vitamin D3, B vitamins and tapioca syrup in the formula and prices from $17.83 a bottle. Treat any page whose “official website” button links back to itself with caution.

Testing and certification

  • Public potency tests: OMNI does not appear in the NOW Foods, SuppCo or James Smith/Eurofins rounds in our lab tracker. Across those rounds, 16 of 28 gummy tests missed their label claim.
  • Brand testing claims: the store says batches are checked by Eurofins for potency and purity and mentions a certificate of analysis. We found no downloadable certificate or lot number on the pages we read.
  • NSF Certified for Sport: the store does not claim it. A third-party listing mentions “NSF-certified manufacturing,” which refers to a facility’s GMP registration, not product certification that tests each lot for potency and banned substances. See which gummies are NSF certified.
  • SuppCo: its listing gives a TrustScore of 8.5 but states SuppCo has not verified product-specific testing.

How to check before you buy: email the brand and ask for the certificate of analysis for the lot number you will receive, showing creatine in grams per serving and the lab name. A real COA names the lab, the method (usually HPLC) and the date. Our safety check guide walks through it.

Marketing claims we could not verify

  • “15% greater strength gains” and “24% faster recovery” at 12 weeks — no study cited for this product.
  • An “8-week women’s trial” with 1.2% body-fat reduction — no study, sample size or citation given.
  • “Made in FDA-approved facilities” — the FDA does not approve supplement facilities or supplements; the same page carries the standard “not evaluated by the FDA” disclaimer.
  • A featured endorser, “Dr. Kirten,” is described differently across pages (nutrition advisor, longevity expert, surgeon), with no verifiable profile.

None of this means the product is bad. It means the claims are marketing, and the creatine research they lean on was done with verified 3–5 g doses of creatine monohydrate, not with this gummy.

Side effects

We found no OMNI-specific safety reports. Expected effects are the same as any creatine gummy: possible stomach upset at large doses, a little water weight in the first weeks, and whatever the gummy base adds (sugar, sweeteners or fibers that bother some stomachs). Because the full ingredient list was not published on the pages we read, people with allergies, diabetes or sugar-alcohol sensitivity should ask the brand first. Questions

How much creatine is in OMNI creatine gummies?

OMNI lists 3 g of creatine monohydrate per 3-gummy serving, about 1 g per gummy. A 5 g dose takes 5 gummies, so a 30-serving pack lasts about 18 days at that dose.

Are OMNI creatine gummies third-party tested?

The brand says Eurofins tests potency and purity, but on October 19, 2026 we found no linked certificate of analysis, no NSF Certified for Sport claim, and no OMNI entry in the three public gummy testing rounds we track.

Do OMNI creatine gummies have side effects?

We found no OMNI-specific safety reports. Expect the usual creatine effects: possible stomach upset at higher doses and some early water weight. The full ingredient and sugar list was not on the store pages we read, so ask the brand if you have allergies or blood-sugar concerns.

Is OMNI creatine legit?

The product is sold by real storefronts, but two different OMNI-named sites show different flavors, prices and review counts, and several marketing claims are unsourced. Ask for a lot-specific certificate of analysis before buying.

Is OMNI or Create better?

Create lists 4.5 g per serving, has NSF Certified for Sport flavors and a published passing lab test, and costs less per gram at subscription prices. OMNI lists 3 g per serving and has no public test result we could find.
