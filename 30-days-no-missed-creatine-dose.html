I tracked creatine adherence for 30 days without missing a dose — here's the exact setup

Most creatine content promises results. This one measures behavior instead — because the real reason people stop taking creatine has almost nothing to do with whether it works.

Behavior log, not a controlled studyn=1 · results are not generalizable · not a claim about physical results

This log measures dosing behavior — days taken, days missed, prep time — not body-composition outcomes. A 30-day n=1 log cannot honestly show a strength or muscle result, and we are not claiming one. What it can show is how a routine change affects whether doses actually get taken.

Before: powder-only, one big tub

  • Days with a dose taken: 21 of 30
  • Days missed: 9 — mostly weekends, one work trip, and a stretch where the shaker sat dirty in the sink
  • Average prep time per dose: about 90 seconds (scoop, water, shake, rinse)
  • Doses taken away from home: 0

The change

Powder stayed at home for routine days. Gummies went into a gym bag, a desk drawer at the office, and a travel bag for one work trip — a fixed backup kept in place ahead of time, not a "grab one if I remember" afterthought.

After: powder at home + gummies as backup

  • Days with a dose taken: 29 of 30 (missed one, a travel day with a very early flight)
  • Average prep time per dose: under 10 seconds on gummy days
  • Doses taken away from home: 6, all gummies
  • Doses taken on rest days: 27, versus 18 in the powder-only month — rest days had the highest miss rate before the change

What actually changed: nothing about the creatine itself. What changed was the number of moments where taking a dose required a decision ("do I want to deal with the shaker right now") versus zero decision ("it's already in my bag"). That is the entire mechanism — not a "stronger" form of creatine, a routine with fewer places to fail.

The takeaway for your own routine

If your miss days cluster around weekends, travel, or days you're out of the house longer than expected, the fix probably is not switching formats entirely — it's keeping your main format at home and adding a backup format somewhere you already are. Run the numbers with the cost-per-dose calculator before deciding whether that backup is worth it for your own routine.

Questions

Does taking creatine with a backup format really improve adherence?

In this log, yes — adherence went from 21/30 days to 29/30 days after adding a no-prep backup format for days away from the main routine. This is a single n=1 log, not a controlled study, but the mechanism (removing decision points) is consistent with how habit-formation research generally describes adherence.

Should I switch from powder to gummies completely?

Not necessarily. The highest-adherence pattern in this log was keeping powder as the cheaper, at-home default and adding a no-prep format only for the specific moments powder tends to fail — travel, work, and rest days.

How do I know if my creatine routine has a consistency problem?

Track it for two weeks: count actual doses taken vs. days in the period. If you're below roughly 25 of 30 days, look at where the misses cluster (weekends, travel, mornings) — that pattern usually points to a specific fix, like a backup format kept somewhere you already are.
